“The code of life is like a Beethoven symphony. We have not yet learned how to write music like that. But evolution does it very well. I am learning how to use evolution to compose new music.” — Frances Arnold

I am a second-year Ph.D. student in Chemical & Biomolecular Engineering at the University of California, Berkeley. My research encompasses the use of modern computational biology pipelines for protein design in bacterial immune systems. Evolution truly is Life’s greatest and most humbling experiment – with 3.8 billion years of history. After all, to borrow the words of Theodosius Dobzhansky, “nothing in biology makes sense except in the light of evolution.” In the same way that Beethoven composed his symphonies, I want to become the composer of Life itself!

Previously, I graduated from the University of California, Davis with a degree in Biochemical Engineering. My undergraduate research work with the McDonald-Nandi Group focused on bioprocess engineering and the characterization of recombinant protein therapeutics in plant expression systems. After graduating from Davis and a brief stint in industry, I worked with Dr. Gianmarc Grazioli’s computational chemistry group at San Jose State University to help develop PyQuiverHS – an open-source web application for calculating kinetic and equilibrium isotope effects.

With this being my personal website, please expect updates on my academic contributions to biomolecular engineering as well as the occasional musings about my wider intellectual interests in the arts.
